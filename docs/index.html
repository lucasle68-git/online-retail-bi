Four questions, four pages

Each page leads with its conclusion and ends with what the business should do about it.

1 of 4 · Is revenue growing?

Revenue rose 2.7% in 2011, all of it in the autumn gift season

£8.78m to £9.01m on the same dates. January–August was flat; September–November grew 6%. Orders fell 4.9% while the average order rose 10.5%, and cancellations almost doubled to 4.8% of sales.

2 of 4 · Which markets and products?

The UK brings 85% of revenue; 22% of products bring 80%

Ireland, the Netherlands and Germany make 55.4% of revenue outside the UK. Two products, cancelled almost in full, account for £246k of the £716k cancelled.

3 of 4 · Which customers matter, and who are we losing?

16% of customers bring 54% of revenue, and 327 of the best have stopped buying

Wholesale-size customers order 4× bigger and 1.7× more often. 711 customers (£1.37m) have gone quiet; the 327 biggest spenders among them hold £1.09m and form a ready-made call list.

4 of 4 · How reliable are these numbers?

Every row is accounted for, and the report matches the database to the penny

91% of removed rows were duplicate copies from two overlapping source files. Five headline numbers are checked against the database on every refresh; one command rebuilds everything in about 30 seconds.

  • Power BI: DAX, 4 pages
  • No row disappears without a reason: numbered cleaning rules, an issue log, and a pipeline that stops if rows or pounds go missing.
  • Missing customer IDs are kept: they carry 13.6% of product revenue, so deleting them would understate sales.
  • Fair comparisons: the data ends on 9 Dec 2011, so 2011 is compared with the same dates in 2010.
  • One definition per metric, built once in SQL and reconciled against every Power BI measure.
